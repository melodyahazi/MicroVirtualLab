Sensitivity Testing

Kirby Bauer Disc Diffusion

Explore the concept of antimicrobial sensitivity testing and learn how the Kirby Bauer disc diffusion method is used to examine the susceptibility of microorganisms to antimicrobial agents.

🎯 Learning Objectives

After completing this experiment, you should be able to:

  • Explain the meaning of susceptibility and sensitivity.
  • Describe the basic concept of agar/disc diffusion testing.
  • Identify the Kirby Bauer method as a disc diffusion method.
  • Explain the meaning of a clear zone around an antimicrobial disc.
  • Recognize the importance of interpreting results using CLSI guidelines.

🧠 Key Concepts

🦠

Susceptibility

Sensitivity or susceptibility refers to testing whether an antimicrobial agent can affect the growth of a microorganism.

💿

Disc Diffusion

In disc diffusion, an antimicrobial agent diffuses from a disc through agar and inhibits microbial growth.

⭕

Clear Zone

A clear zone around an antimicrobial disc represents inhibition of growth and indicates susceptibility.

📏

Zone Diameter

Disc diffusion results involve reading the diameter of the inhibition zone and interpreting the result using appropriate guidelines.

🔬 Methods of Sensitivity Testing

01

Agar / Disc Diffusion

An antimicrobial agent diffuses from a disc or strip through agar. Inhibition of growth can appear as a clear zone around the disc.

Kirby Bauer Method
02

Tube Dilution

Tube dilution is another method used to determine the sensitivity of microorganisms to antimicrobial agents.
