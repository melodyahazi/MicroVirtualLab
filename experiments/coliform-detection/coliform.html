Detection of Coliforms and Indicator Organisms

This virtual learning activity introduces the concepts used in the detection of coliforms and other indicator microorganisms, with emphasis on water quality and microbial population estimation.

Learning focus: Indicator microorganisms, coliforms, faecal indicators, total count, membrane filtration, multiple-tube testing, MPN, and interpretation of microbial population estimates.

Learning Objectives

After completing this activity, you should be able to:

01

Identify Indicator Microorganisms

Explain the role of indicator microorganisms in assessing water quality.

02

Understand Coliforms

Describe the general characteristics of the coliform group.

03

Understand MPN

Explain the purpose of the Most Probable Number approach for estimating microbial populations.

04

Recognize Testing Methods

Distinguish between total count, membrane filtration, and multiple-tube methods.

Key Concepts

🦠

Coliforms

The manual describes coliforms as a group of Gram-negative, non-spore-forming rod-shaped bacteria with defined characteristics used in water-quality examination.

🔎

Indicator Organisms

Indicator organisms provide useful information about microbial quality and possible pollution of water.

📊

MPN

Most Probable Number is an estimation approach used to estimate microbial population sizes using statistical interpretation.

💧

Water Quality

Microbiological examination of water can provide information about its microbial quality and possible pollution.

Major Indicator Groups

The manual identifies three major groups of faecal pollution indicators.

1️⃣

Coliform Group

The manual gives Escherichia coli as a representative organism of this group.

2️⃣

Faecal Streptococci

The manual identifies Streptococcus faecalis as a representative organism.

3️⃣

Sulphite-Reducing Clostridia

The manual identifies Clostridium perfringens as a representative organism.

Water Examination Methods

A

Total Count

Also called colony count or standard plate count. It provides an estimate of the total number of microorganisms represented in a sample.

B

Membrane Filtration

A microbiological examination method identified by the manual for bacteriological examination of water.

C

Multiple-Tube Method

Also associated with the Most Probable Number approach and based on statistical interpretation of results.

D

MPN Interpretation

MPN provides an estimate of microbial population size rather than a direct count of individual microorganisms.
